MODEL DOCUMENTATION

How we think.

The assumptions, limitations and arithmetic behind each ranking. A model should be auditable before it is persuasive.

Download formula-based Excel ↧
01 / BUSINESS ECONOMICS

Margins tend to revert — but not always

Competitive forces and business cycles may pull unusually high or low operating margins toward a longer-run level. Structural changes, acquisitions and new competitive advantages can prevent this from happening.

02 / NORMALIZED MARGINS

Three anchors for earning power

60% company median + 25% peer average + 15% universe median

The default lookback is ten fiscal years. Company and peer group histories are partially pooled; companies with insufficient observations are excluded.

03 / PERSISTENCE

How quickly could margins recover?

mₜ = m* + φᵗ (m₀ − m*)

A company-specific AR(1) estimate is shrunk toward a persistence prior of 0.60. The estimate describes a stylized recovery path, not a guarantee.

04 / VALUATION

Two separate valuation questions

The margin-fade DCF compares recovering margins against a scenario in which today's margin persists. A separate market-implied margin compares normalized profitability with what the current enterprise value appears to require. These measures can be correlated and should not be read as independent evidence.

05 / SIGNALS & TRADING

Repeatable rules, not predictions

The model combines margin gap (30%), inverted history z-score (15%), implied-margin gap (25%) and margin-reversion upside (30%). Eligible top-quintile names are LONG, the bottom quintile AVOID, and deteriorating low-margin companies are WATCH. The five highest eligible positive-upside candidates enter the paper portfolio, with a 30% weight cap and monthly rebalance.

06 / EVIDENCE

Test the claim that matters

An in-sample regression of next-year margin changes provides context for mean reversion. It does not demonstrate that the strategy earns abnormal stock returns. Proper performance validation requires point-in-time financial histories, adjusted prices including corporate actions, delisted companies, realistic execution lag and out-of-sample testing.
